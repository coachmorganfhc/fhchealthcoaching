CoachRx Integration

CoachRx handles programming, macros, tracking. This portal handles the relationship layer.

  • Weekly automated check-in — edit per client every week. Never generic. Always tuned to current pillar focus.
  • 2–3 touchpoints minimum — every client, every week. Replies to CoachRx updates. The relationship-builder.
  • Programming & macro adjustments — inside CoachRx, in line with assessment & session updates.
  • Cross-coach visibility — for combined health + fitness clients, both coaches review each other's logs before sessions.
The 12-Week Chapter

Every 12 weeks, a deliberate review.

Ongoing coaching needs structure or it drifts. 12-week chapters — each ending with a dedicated review that pulls back from the week-to-week and looks at the arc.
DURING THE 12 WEEKS
  • · Biweekly or monthly sessions (booked in advance)
  • · Mon/Wed/Fri communication rhythm
  • · 2–3 CoachRx touchpoints per week
  • · Programming evolves inside CoachRx
AT THE 12-WEEK REVIEW
  • · Visual progress report (data + photos + reflections)
  • · What worked, what shifted, what surprised
  • · Recalibrate goals for the next 12 weeks
  • · Updated programming roadmap delivered

Session cadence — biweekly vs. monthly

Biweekly sessions are the default for active change phases — recomp, performance build, behavior overhaul. Higher frequency holds the work.

Monthly sessions for maintenance-phase clients who have integrated their practices. Don't drop cadence too early — wait for the client to ask, then validate readiness.

Booking rule: the next session is always confirmed in the calendar before this one ends. The tracker enforces this in the Next Booked column.

Scenario Library

Coach Playbook

The moments that test us most —
met with structure and humanity.
Scenario · 01
When a client hits a plateau
  1. Don't rush to fix. Plateaus are often biological — and emotional.
  2. Pull the last 4 weeks of CoachRx data. Look for compliance, not just outcomes.
  3. Reflect back what they've already changed. Most clients underestimate.
  4. Examine sleep & stress before nutrition or training.
  5. Propose a 14-day experiment, not a forever plan.
  6. Send the "Stuck Doesn't Mean Stopped" message from the library.
Scenario · 02
When CoachRx logs go quiet
  1. One miss = curiosity. Two = compassionate check-in. Three = direct conversation.
  2. Never lead with "you haven't logged." Lead with concern.
  3. Use the gentle-pulse template — never make them feel guilty.
  4. Ask what's getting in the way of logging. Listen.
  5. Offer a lower-friction option (voice notes, photo logs) before they disengage.
  6. If they need to pause, hold the door open. Don't make them justify it.
Scenario · 03
The 12-week review session
  1. Build the visual progress report 5 days before. Photos, data, reflections.
  2. Send the report ahead of session — never reveal it cold.
  3. Open with what surprised you. Then ask what surprised them.
  4. "What got harder? What got easier?" — sit in both answers.
  5. Recalibrate the next 12 weeks together. Not a renewal sell — a re-aiming.
  6. Send a personal "Distance Travelled" note within 48 hours.
Scenario · 04
When a client shares something hard
  1. Stop coaching. Start listening. Sit in the silence.
  2. "Thank you for trusting me with that" — before anything else.
  3. Resist the urge to immediately reframe or solve.
  4. Ask: "What would feel most supportive right now?"
  5. Know your scope. If clinical: refer, warmly, with a name.
  6. Follow up within 24 hours, no agenda — just a hand on the shoulder.
Scenario · 05
Dropping cadence — biweekly to monthly
  1. Wait for the client to ask. Don't propose it yourself first.
  2. Validate readiness: integration of practices, self-sufficiency, stability.
  3. Frame as a graduation, not a downgrade. Words matter here.
  4. Increase Mon/Wed/Fri communications intentionally to fill the gap.
  5. Keep CoachRx touchpoints at 2–3 minimum. Cadence drop doesn't change that.
  6. Revisit at the next 12-week review — biweekly may need to return.
Scenario · 06
When a client wants to pause or leave
  1. Never take it personally. Sometimes the work is complete.
  2. Ask the honest exit question: "What was missing for you?"
  3. Thank them for the trust — specifically, with details.
  4. Offer a closing session. Make completion ceremonial.
  5. Send a "What you taught me" note within 7 days.
  6. Keep the door open without making it weird.

A note on protocols

These are scaffolding, not scripts. Use them when you're tired, when you're new, when you've been blindsided. Trust your judgment to depart from them when the moment asks for something else.
